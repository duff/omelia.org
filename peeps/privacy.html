Peeps Privacy Policy

Effective October 17, 2026

Peeps is a desktop address book for your Google contacts. It runs on your computer. There is no Peeps server, and the developer never receives, sees, or stores your data.

What Peeps accesses

When you connect a Google account, Peeps asks Google for access to your contacts, using the scope https://www.googleapis.com/auth/contacts. With it, Peeps reads your contacts and their details, such as names, email addresses, phone numbers, postal addresses, birthdays, notes, and photos. It adds, changes, and deletes contacts only when you ask it to.

Where your data is kept

Everything Peeps keeps stays on your computer:

  • A copy of your contacts, so search is fast, in ~/.local/share/peeps
  • Contact photos in ~/.cache/peeps
  • Settings in ~/.config/peeps
  • The Google sign-in token in your computer's login keyring
  • vCards made when you share a contact, in a temporary folder that is cleared when you log out

How your data is protected

  • Encrypted in transit. Peeps talks to Google only over HTTPS, so your contacts and your sign-in travel encrypted with TLS.
  • Secure sign-in. You sign in on Google's own page in your browser, so Peeps never sees your Google password. Sign-in uses OAuth 2.0 with PKCE, and Google's reply comes back only to your own computer, at 127.0.0.1.
  • Encrypted sign-in token. The token Google gives Peeps is kept only in your computer's login keyring, which stores it encrypted. Peeps never writes it to an ordinary file.
  • Private files. The copy of your contacts, your settings, the vCards Peeps saves, and contact photos can be read only by your own user account on the computer.
  • Nothing held by the developer. There is no Peeps server or database, so there is no central copy of anyone's contacts to be breached.
  • Open source. Peeps' source code is public, so anyone can check how it handles your data.

Peeps does not add its own encryption to the files it keeps on your computer. To protect them if your computer is lost or stolen, use full-disk encryption.

Who Peeps talks to

Peeps connects only to Google: to sign you in, and to read and save your contacts and photos through the Google People API. It has no analytics, no tracking, and no ads. When you choose to share a contact, by email or with LocalSend, that contact goes where you send it.

Sharing and selling

Peeps does not sell, rent, or share your data with anyone. Your data is handled only by Peeps on your computer and by Google. It is never used for advertising or to train AI models.

Google API Services User Data Policy

Peeps' use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Keeping and removing your data

Peeps keeps its copy of your contacts while your Google account is connected, and keeps it current: each sync with Google brings in your changes and removes contacts you have deleted in Google. vCards made for sharing are deleted when you log out.

In Peeps, Menu → Google account → Disconnect deletes the sign-in and the copy of your contacts from your computer. To remove everything, also delete the folders listed above. To revoke Peeps' access on Google's side, visit your Google Account's third-party connections. Your contacts themselves stay in your Google account.
